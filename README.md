# CRYSTALs-Dilithium

In the era of advancing quantum cryptography, the security of classical cryptographic systems faces threats. Due to that reason, the post quantum cryptography field emerged, seeking to develop encryption techniques resistant to quantum algorithms. One promising solution is the Dilithium-CRYSTALs algorithm, which is a lattice-based crypto-algorithm, which has not been able to be broken yet.

To achieve efficient security, the need of hardware accelerators has emerged, due to the Post-Quantum Cryptography algorithm’s complexity and mathematical operations. This complexity is double-edged sword, as on one hand, it ensured resistance to quantum attacks, but on the other hand, it can lead to a slow performance when implemented on conventional hardware. Therefore, hardware accelerators specialized for these algorithms are essential to maintain acceptable levels of efficiency and performance. These accelerators can improve the speed and efficiency of the cryptographic operations, while ensuring that secure communication remains possible in the face of evolving threats posed by quantum computers.

The purpose of this thesis is the high-level synthesis of the post quantum cryptography Dilithium-CRYSTALs algorithm and its optimization in terms of latency and resource consumption. For this purpose, the Vivado HLS tool from Xilinx was used, and more specifically we chose the FPGA platform of kintex-7 family.. First, the algorithm is imported into the Vivado HLS tool where its synthesis is implemented, which results in an RTL description. Then a series of optimizations are applied through the techniques supported by this tool in order to produce the best possible result. The final implementation is judged in terms of latency reduction and the use of hardware resources required. Finally, there was comparison between our implementation with that of our referenced one and we concluded that our implementation achieved 3 to 4 times less latency.
